# A solar eclipse map

The maps follow the ones in the *Five Millennium Canon of Solar Eclipses*: an outline of where
the eclipse can be seen, the narrow path through it, and the moment of greatest eclipse.

## The curves

| Line | What it marks |
| ---- | ------------- |
| Solid green, the penumbral limits | The northern and southern edges of the eclipse. Beyond them the Moon misses the Sun altogether. |
| Solid green, the sunrise and sunset curves | Where the eclipse begins or ends with the Sun on the horizon. |
| Magenta | Where the maximum of the eclipse happens at sunrise or sunset. |
| Dashed green | Magnitude 0.5 at maximum: half the Sun's diameter covered. |
| Solid blue | The limits of the path of totality or annularity. |
| Dashed blue | The central line, where the shadow's axis meets the ground. |

Two marks stand on the map. The star is the point of greatest eclipse, and the cross is where
the Sun stands overhead at that moment.

As the time bar moves, the grey hemisphere is night, the dashed grey ring is the edge of the
penumbra on the ground, and the dark spot inside it is the umbra.

## The numbers

**Greatest eclipse** is the moment the shadow's axis passes closest to the Earth's centre. It
is a single instant for the whole eclipse, and the place under it is not always the place with
the longest totality.

**Gamma** is that closest distance in Earth equatorial radii, positive when the axis passes
north of the centre. A gamma near 0 is a shadow crossing the middle of the Earth's disc. A gamma
near 1 is a shadow grazing its edge.

**Magnitude** is the fraction of the Sun's diameter covered at the point of greatest eclipse.
Above 1 the Moon looks larger than the Sun and the eclipse is total. Below 1 in a central
eclipse the Moon looks smaller and leaves a ring, so the eclipse is annular.

The eclipse of 2027 February 6 has gamma -0.2951 and magnitude 0.9281. Its ring lasts 7m51s at
greatest eclipse, on a path 282 km wide, with the Sun 73° up.

## A path under a low Sun

Compare the eclipse of 2026 August 12, whose gamma is 0.8977:

The axis passes far from the Earth's centre, so the shadow arrives at a slant. At greatest
eclipse the Sun is only 26° up, and the path spreads over more ground than the same shadow
would on a Sun overhead.

## A place

On the full page, click the globe or give a latitude and longitude to see the eclipse from
there: its contacts, the Sun's altitude at each, the magnitude, and the share of the Sun's disc
covered. The contacts are named C1 and C4 for the start and end of the partial phase, and C2
and C3 for the start and end of totality or annularity.

Magnitude and obscuration are different measures. Magnitude is a share of the Sun's diameter,
and obscuration is a share of its area, so obscuration is the smaller of the two in a partial
eclipse.
