# Against the records

The Canons are computed, so agreeing with them shows only that two computations agree. The
older test is what people saw.

Before about 1600, ΔT itself is known only from records like these. A record that an eclipse
was total somewhere therefore can't check a path on its own. It is tested as the claim it
supports: that the place lay in the path for some ΔT within twice the model's uncertainty.

## Timed observations

| Record | Observed | Allowed |
| ------ | -------- | ------- |
| Halley, London, 1715 May 3 | Totality 3m23s, by a pendulum clock | 5 s; contacts within 30 s of his true time |
| Königsberg, 1851 July 28, Berkowski's first photograph of totality | Totality 2m56s | 3 s |
| Columbus, Jamaica, 1504 March 1, lunar | Totality 48 minutes, by an hourglass | 1.5 minutes, with the Moon above his horizon |

Halley's account of the 1715 eclipse is a good one to read: the immersion at 9h 9m 3s and the
emersion at 9h 12m 26s true time, from the Royal Society's house in Crane Court. It tests
the duration, which ΔT barely touches, and the contacts, which guard against a gross error in
the Earth's rotation.

## Old records

**The eclipse of Bur-Sagale, -762 June 15.** The Assyrian eponym list says that in the month
Simanu an eclipse of the Sun took place. It doesn't say total. Nineveh is checked for a deep
partial phase, above magnitude 0.9 at the model's ΔT, and for lying in the path within the
model's uncertainty.

**The eclipse of Thales, -584 May 28.** Herodotus says that as the battle between the Lydians
and the Medes grew warm, day was suddenly changed into night. He names no battlefield, and the
Halys, the border of the two kingdoms, is a modern placing. A stretch of central Anatolia is
checked: the eclipse is total somewhere in it for a ΔT within the model's uncertainty.

**Babylon, -135 April 15.** The tablets record Venus, Mercury and the Normal Stars visible, and
Jupiter and Mars seen during the eclipse. Stars by day mean totality, and Babylon is in the path
for a ΔT within the model's uncertainty.

## The bounds on ΔT

Stephenson, Morrison and Hohenkerk (2016) tabulate, for each place where the Sun was recorded
as totally eclipsed, the range of ΔT for which that place lay in the path. Those ranges are
what bound ΔT before the telescope.

Navigate's ΔT is that paper's spline, reduced with a Moon close to DE431's. With the same kind
of Moon, each place must lie in the computed path over the same range, to within 40 seconds.
A Moon running ahead of or behind the one used for the records would shift both ends of every
range together, so this tests the Moon as well as ΔT.
