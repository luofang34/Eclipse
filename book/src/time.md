# Time and ΔT

Two clocks run through every eclipse, and the difference between them is the largest
uncertainty in the old ones.

**Terrestrial Time (TT)**, or TD in the Canons, is the uniform time the Sun and Moon move in.
The shadow's motion through space is computed in TT.

**Universal Time (UT1)** follows the turning Earth. Where the shadow lands depends on how far
the Earth had turned, and that is a matter of UT1.

**ΔT** is TT - UT1. The Earth's rotation is slowing and irregular, so ΔT cannot be derived from
theory. It has to be measured.

## Where ΔT comes from

The page shows ΔT with its uncertainty and its basis for each eclipse. Navigate takes it from
Stephenson, Morrison and Hohenkerk (2016) before 1948, from USNO and IERS data since, and from a
model beyond them.

ΔT is what moves an old eclipse across the map without changing the eclipse. Open the eclipse of
-584 May 28, the one Thales is said to have foretold:

<figure class="eclipse" data-query="date=-0584-05-28" data-title="The total solar eclipse of -584 May 28"></figure>

The Canon gives ΔT as 18,384 seconds here, about five hours. The Earth turns 15.041 arcseconds
in a second, so an hour's error in ΔT moves the path 15° of longitude along the map. The shadow
in TT stays where it was, and it is the Earth under it that moves.

That is why this book's chapter on [the records](records.md) tests old eclipses as claims about
ΔT, not as predictions of where a path fell.

## The near future

ΔT is uncertain ahead as well as behind. For the eclipse of 2027 February 6, the Canon's model
gives 76 seconds and the page's IERS prediction gives 69.3 seconds. Greatest eclipse stays at
16:00:48 TD in both, and the page reads it as 15:59:38 UT.

The difference of 6.7 seconds turns the Earth by 0.028°, a few kilometres at most. That matters
at the edge of a path and hardly at all on the map.

## Calendars

The Canons date eclipses in the Julian calendar before 1582 October 15 and in the Gregorian
from then, with astronomical year numbers, and the page does the same.
